The Problem

That ache at the end of the day is not your age. It is the floor you stand on.

Your feet were never built for eight, ten, twelve hours on hard flat ground. Here is what happens when they get it anyway.

  • Hard ground gives nothing back. Concrete, tile and vinyl do not give at all. You put force down with every step, and all of it comes straight back up.
  • If the shoe does not absorb it, your foot does. The band of tissue along the bottom of your foot takes the hit first.
  • It does not reset overnight. Load it all day and it is still sore in the morning. That is why the first steps out of bed are the worst ones.
  • Then it travels. You start walking differently to protect it, and your knees, hips and lower back pay for that.
In short

This is not wear and tear you age into. It is a question of what sits between your foot and the floor, and that is the part you can change today.

The Fix · The dual-density midsole

How do I know these will fit?

[vrsn] runs true to size, so order the US size you normally wear. If you are between sizes, take the size up. The size guide in the order section has US, EU and UK side by side, and exchanges are free for 100 days, so a swap costs you nothing but the time.

What sizes do you make?

US Women's 6 to 12.5 and US Men's 4 to 11, in white, navy, beige and all black. Every pair has the wide toe box and the removable insole as standard.

I have wide feet or a bunion. Do I need to size up?

No. Order your usual size. The width you need is in the toe box already, which is the part most shoes get wrong. Sizing up to find room usually leaves your heel sliding instead.

Is there a break-in period?

No. The arch support is working the first time you stand up in them, which is also why the 100-day trial is a fair test from day one rather than from week three.

I have tried insoles and they did nothing. Why is this different?

An insole sits on top of whatever the shoe already was. If the sole underneath is flat foam that packs down, you have added a layer to a shoe that still is not sharing the load. Here the arch shape and the two densities are the sole itself.

I already wear custom orthotics. Do they fit?

Yes. The insole lifts fully out, so your own orthotics drop in without trimming anything. If a podiatrist prescribed them, keep wearing them.

Will they feel like an orthopedic shoe?

No. It is a low-profile sneaker in four colours, and the support is inside the sole rather than announced on the outside of it. That matters more than it sounds: the pair that looks like a normal shoe is the pair that gets worn every day.

I have been in barefoot or zero-drop shoes. Will these feel bulky?

No, and you can switch straight over with no transition protocol. You keep the wide toe box and the flex at the front. What changes is that the midsole takes the impact your foot has been absorbing on concrete, which is usually what was causing the injuries.

Will they help with knee or lower back pain?

Many people report that they do, because the chain starts at the floor. We will not promise it. If your knees or back are the main problem, see a professional as well, and treat the shoes as the part of it you control.

Are they good for flat feet or high arches?

The arch is contoured rather than locked to one shape, which is why it suits a range of feet. If you have a very high arch or a diagnosed structural issue, your own orthotics still go in on top.

Can I use them for walking all day, or for running?

All-day walking and standing is exactly what they are built for: shifts, airports, 20,000-step days. They are not a dedicated running shoe, so keep running trainers for running.

How long do they last?

With daily wear most people get a year or more out of a pair, which is the other reason a second pair helps: rotating two makes both last longer than wearing one into the ground.

Can I wash them?

Yes. Take the insoles out, cold gentle cycle, then air dry. Do not put them in the dryer.

Do I need to wear socks with them?

No. The knit lining is smooth enough to wear barefoot, and it washes, which is what makes that practical.

Are they slippery on wet floors?

No. The outsole is slip-resistant and grips on wet tile, wood and concrete, which is the point on hospital and kitchen floors.

Only if you want more. The shoe already has a contoured arch and a removable insole. The add-on is a firmer footbed with a deeper heel cup, which some people prefer if their mornings are the worst part of the day. It is size-matched to your first pair automatically.
